Netflix, Max, Disney+ and Prime Video: how each handles removals

By StaticReel · Nov 5, 2026

"It left streaming" means different things depending on the service. The business model behind each catalog changes how often titles disappear and how much warning you get. These are general patterns, not guarantees, and individual titles vary.

Netflix

Netflix mixes its own productions with a large licensed library. Licensed films and older series rotate out regularly, and Netflix usually announces upcoming exits. Its own originals are less likely to leave, though they can. For the licensing background, see why shows leave Netflix.

Max

Max draws heavily on the catalog of its parent company, which makes some of its library more stable than a pure licensing model. It still carries films from outside studios, and those can rotate. Max tends to list arrivals more prominently than departures, so check dates before starting a long series.

Disney+

Disney+ leans on Disney's own catalog, so fewer titles disappear because a license ended. Changes are often additions, scheduled premieres and moves between Disney's services. If you pay for Disney+ specifically, confirm the service name next to a date, because Disney publishes announcements that cover several of its services at once.

Prime Video

Prime Video combines titles included with Prime and titles that cost extra to rent or buy. A movie "leaving Prime" may simply move from included to paid. When you see a removal, check whether it is gone entirely or just no longer free with your subscription.

What to do with this

  • On any service, check the date before starting a long series.
  • On Netflix, expect more movement near the start of each month.
  • On Prime Video, confirm whether a title is included or paid.
