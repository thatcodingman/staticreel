Why some recent movies leave Netflix within weeks

By StaticReel · Nov 5, 2026

It seems odd: a movie from last year arrives on Netflix and is gone a few weeks later. That is usually not a mistake. It is how first-window licensing works.

What a first window is

After a film's theatrical run, a studio often grants one streaming service the first chance to show it for a limited period. When that period ends, the film can move to another service, a different pay window, or a free ad-supported channel. Some lists mark these titles with the studio, such as "Sony first-window" or "Universal first-window."

Examples this month

This November's removal lists include several recent films that appear to follow this pattern, such as Heart Eyes, Paddington in Peru, I'm Still Here and M3GAN 2.0. The labeling comes from What's on Netflix's removal list, so these entries are marked Corroborated in StaticReel rather than Verified.

What it means for viewers

  • A recent movie on Netflix may have a shorter stay than an older catalog film.
  • If a film leaves, it may reappear on another service, so check before assuming it is gone for good.
  • Look at the date. A title that arrived recently may already have an exit date.

Why it is hard to predict

These contracts are private, so the length of a window is rarely announced in advance. The reliable signal is the removal notice itself. See where to find official announcements, and the Netflix November guide for the full list.
