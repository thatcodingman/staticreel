Is that streaming date reliable? A quick checklist

By StaticReel · Nov 5, 2026

Every week a date goes viral: a show "arrives Friday" or a movie "leaves on the 15th." Some of those dates are solid. Others are guesses repeated until they look like facts. Here is how we sort them, and how you can too.

1. Who published it first?

The most reliable source is the service itself: a press release, a "coming soon" page, or an official social post. A news article that links to one of those is a good sign. An article that links only to other articles is a chain, and every link in it may be copying the first.

2. Is the date specific?

"November" and "this fall" are intentions. "November 12" is a commitment. Exact dates are more reliable, but only when they come from the right source.

3. Is it the right service?

Shared press pages often cover more than one service. A title that arrives on a sister service is easy to mistake for the one you pay for. Check the service name next to the date.

4. Is the date still current?

Dates move, especially premieres. A listing from a few months ago may describe a schedule that has since changed. Look at when the source was last updated.

5. Does it say what time zone or region?

Arrival times and even days can differ outside the U.S. A title that "arrives Friday" in the U.S. may show up a day earlier or later elsewhere.

How StaticReel handles this

We put these questions into a label. Dates we read on the service's own page are Verified. Dates reported by reliable outlets are Corroborated. Leads we cannot source are Unconfirmed. The full method is in how we verify streaming changes.
