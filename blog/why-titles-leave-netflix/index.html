Why movies and shows leave Netflix (and why it is usually the first of the month)

By StaticReel · Nov 4, 2026

Almost everyone has had the experience: you add a movie to your list, come back a month later, and it is gone. Nothing broke. The title left because of how streaming rights work.

Netflix often rents what it shows

Netflix makes some of what it streams, but a large share of its catalog is licensed from studios and distributors. A license is a contract with an end date. When the date arrives, the rights revert to the owner unless both sides renew. Renewal is a business decision, and it depends on price, what else the studio wants to do with the title, and how much Netflix subscribers actually watched it.

Why removals bunch up on the 1st

Many license terms are written to start and end at the boundary of a month, which makes the accounting simpler. That is why a long list of films tends to disappear on the 1st, and why the 1st is also a big day for new arrivals. Mid-month removals do happen, usually for individual series or for licenses signed on a different schedule.

Originals are different, but not permanent

A title Netflix produced itself does not have a licensing clock in the same way, so it tends to stay. There are exceptions, such as a show whose rights were shared with a studio or a title Netflix chooses to retire. If you are tracking a specific original, the signs to watch are a cancellation or end-of-run announcement rather than a leaving date.

What this means for you

  • If it is a licensed movie from a major studio, assume it may rotate out within a year or two.
  • If it is a multi-season series you have not started, check the leaving date before you begin.
  • Netflix posts its own upcoming removals, which is the source we use for confirmed dates.
